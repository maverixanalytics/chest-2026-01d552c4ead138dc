Narwhal Cryo System — tips & tricks

Field reference for reps supporting mechanical cryobiopsy demos at CHEST — inspection, setup, operation, and the talking points physicians ask about most.

01Pre-use inspection and watchouts

  • Check the probe shaft for kinks or damage.
  • Actuate the handle to short and long, confirming ~3 mm and ~6 mm exposed at the distal end. If the lengths don't appear, re-check for a kink or bend.
  • Remove and discard the packaging lock (red on clinical units). It holds the probe sheathed during shipping.
  • The cartridge ships non-sterile in a foil moisture-resistant pouch; the probe is sterile. Inspect the pouch for tears or holes.
Watchouts
  • Always remove the probe from the tray and uncoil it before actuating tip lengths. Coiled, friction between probe and sheath is high enough to kink the probe inside the handle.
  • Each length setting has a tactile and audible click. Don't leave the device between lengths.
  • Once the pouch is open, the molecular sieve begins absorbing moisture — use the cartridge within 1–2 hours. Beyond that there's risk of ice clogs (no safety risk, but N2O won't flow). Don't reseal the pouch to save a cartridge; moisture exposure has already occurred.

02Prep

  • Screw the cartridge into the housing with no gap between the cartridge flange and the top of the housing.
  • Hang the cartridge from the IV pole suspended vertically, so liquid rather than gaseous N2O flows into the system. If it isn't vertical, cooling power drops — no safety concern, the device just won't freeze well.
  • Prime the system: expose the short or long tip, place it in saline, and run the cryo up to 10 seconds. Confirm an audible whistle, an ice ball forming, and no bubbles escaping from the tip.
Note
In the sheathed position the on/off switch can't be fully clicked to "On." This is by design, not a malfunction.

03Operation

  • Each probe is qualified for up to two cartridges. A third or fourth wasn't tested, so we can't recommend it to clinicians.
  • Each cartridge is qualified up to 80 seconds of freeze time.
  • Never adjust probe length while freezing. The probe and sheath stick together during freeze — shifting length then subjects the probe to excessive force and risks kinking it inside the handle.
Whistle
  • Its primary function is to indicate that cryo is on — most importantly, once the probe and biopsy are out of the patient, it's the reminder to switch off and conserve freeze time.
  • The sound can vary: it may warble or quiet toward the end of the procedure. Encourage physicians not to read into the variability — the device still works effectively for the full 80 seconds per cartridge. If biopsy sizes feel smaller, they can add 1–2 seconds of freeze time.
Probe tip
  • Adjustable length. Short is ~3 mm (similar to Erbe), long is ~6 mm. Our animal study experience indicates no increased safety risk on the long setting — no pneumothorax or bleeding complications observed. Useful framing: the long setting makes a longer iceball, not a larger-diameter one. Dr. Hogarth comically calls it a cream puff vs. an éclair.
  • Pointed tip. Engages tissue directly compared with a blunt tip and may not require pretracking with a needle, though there's no official data behind that. It does require extra attention for tumor debulking, clot extraction, and foreign body extraction versus a blunt tip.
  • A scraper for biopsy removal is packaged with the probe, very similar to Erbe.

04End of procedure

Every component is disposable. Unscrew the cartridge from the housing to release remaining N2O pressure — never discard the device without releasing pressure first.

Note
N2O expels from the bottom of the cartridge. Keep that opening clear and pointed away from people and equipment.

05Potential talking points

If asked about CO2 vs. N2O

Erbe and Endocision use CO2; Narwhal uses N2O. There's no noticeable performance difference — we tuned Narwhal's iceball performance to be similar to Erbe — and no additional safety considerations with N2O.

If asked about the Erbe recall
“Erbe USA has reported instances of the affected Cryoprobes rupturing or bursting during activation. The rupture is caused by insufficient adhesive being applied during production, leading to excessive input pressure.”FDA website

We perform 100% pressure and flow testing before packaging, on every device manufactured, to confirm the cryogen fluid pathway is functioning properly. That should increase confidence that we won't see a similar failure, though it can never be guaranteed.
